# Report — SciFact retrieval and verification (all numbers: validation split)

**Q1.** BM25 wins on 673 (1.00 vs 0.36) and 1261 (1.00 vs 0.63): both claims contain rare exact terms that also appear in the gold title ("LDL-cholesterol", "reactive oxygen species", "genomic instability"); bge-base drifts to topically similar but broader reviews ("A Century of Cholesterol and Coronaries"). bge-base wins on 766 (1.00 vs 0.00; BM25 rank 67) and 1311 (1.00 vs 0.00): the gold abstract paraphrases the claim with no shared key word ("medications" ↔ "pharmacotherapy"; "lncRNAs" ↔ "LincRNA-p21", "trans-acting factors"). Overall 14 claims favour BM25, 16 favour bge-base, 76 tie.

**Q2.** Fusion. Min-max interpolation of BM25 (abstracts expanded with training claims), bge-base and MedCPT gave nDCG@10 0.949 vs 0.928 for the best single system (+0.021); without expansion 0.915 vs 0.898. Cost: ≈290 ms/query on CPU instead of 152 ms. Reranking added little: a general cross-encoder (bge-reranker-base) *hurt* (0.870), preferring related studies over the evidence. MedCPT-Cross-Encoder fed the citing training claims reached 0.956 (+0.007, bootstrap p=0.28), at 9.1 s/query on CPU, about 30× the cost.

**Q3.** Yes. Fine-tuning bge-small (0.870 zero-shot) with hard negatives (BM25 ∪ base-model ranks 3–30, gold removed) gave 0.899, versus 0.882 with random negatives. I tracked validation nDCG@10 and nDCG@10 on 100 training claims after every epoch. Validation was still rising at epoch 4, so there was no clear overfitting, but a widening train–val gap (0.960 vs 0.899) and Recall@100 dropping 1.000 → 0.991 were early warning signs; the best epoch is selected on validation.

**Q4.** 4 of 45 validation error claims (9%) are retrieval_miss. Final verifier: the cross-encoder picks evidence among the hybrid's top 3, an NLI ensemble assigns the label. The rest: entity_mismatch 15 (mostly NEI claims where we still predicted an abstract), other 12, needs_multiple_docs 8, numerical 4, negation 2. Moving evidence selection from NLI confidence to the cross-encoder cut errors from 73 to 45 and raised F1 from 0.627 to 0.741 (5-fold CV 0.741). Retrieval is not the bottleneck.

**Q5.** Fine-tune the MedCPT cross-encoder on training claims (gold abstract vs hard negatives from our hybrid), because it now decides which abstracts count as evidence: it separates evidence from non-evidence far better than NLI (pair AUC 0.96 vs 0.68), and its remaining mistakes (false alarms on NEI claims, missed second documents) are the largest error groups. It is used zero-shot today. The same model would also improve t3_rerank, which is currently only +0.007 over the hybrid.
