# Report — SciFact retrieval and verification (all numbers: validation split)

**Q1.** BM25 wins on 673 (1.00 vs 0.36) and 1261 (1.00 vs 0.63): both claims contain rare exact terms that also appear in the gold title ("LDL-cholesterol", "reactive oxygen species", "genomic instability"); bge-base drifts to topically similar but broader reviews ("A Century of Cholesterol and Coronaries"). bge-base wins on 766 (1.00 vs 0.00; BM25 rank 67) and 1311 (1.00 vs 0.00): the gold abstract paraphrases the claim with no shared key word ("medications" ↔ "pharmacotherapy"; "lncRNAs" ↔ "LincRNA-p21", "trans-acting factors"). Overall 14 claims favour BM25, 16 favour bge-base, 76 tie.

**Q2.** Fusion. Min-max interpolation of BM25 (abstracts expanded with training claims), bge-base and MedCPT gave nDCG@10 0.949 vs 0.928 for the best single system (+0.021); without expansion 0.915 vs 0.898. Cost: ≈290 ms/query on CPU instead of 152 ms. Reranking added less: a general cross-encoder (bge-reranker-base) *hurt* (0.870). MedCPT-Cross-Encoder fed the citing training claims reached 0.956, and 0.968 after fine-tuning it on training claims (+0.019 over hybrid, p=0.10), at 9.2 s/query on CPU, about 30× the cost.

**Q3.** Yes. Fine-tuning bge-base (0.877 before training, documents cut to 256 tokens) gave 0.920 with hard negatives (BM25 ∪ base-model ranks 3–30, gold removed) vs 0.906 with random negatives; bge-small showed the same pattern (0.899 vs 0.882). Overfitting was clear: with random negatives, validation nDCG@10 peaked after epoch 1 (0.906) and fell to 0.891 while nDCG on 100 training claims kept rising (0.960 → 0.972). I detected it by evaluating validation and a training subset after every epoch, and kept the best validation epoch.

**Q4.** 2 of 40 validation error claims (5%) are retrieval_miss. Final verifier: two cross-encoders pick evidence among the t3_rerank top 3, an NLI ensemble assigns the label (F1 0.763, 5-fold CV 0.763; NLI alone 0.627). Others: other 13, entity_mismatch 11, numerical 6, needs_multiple_docs 6, negation 2. By outcome: 11 claims miss all evidence, 11 miss a second abstract or add an extra one, 10 are false alarms on NEI claims, only 6 have the wrong label. Retrieval is not the bottleneck; deciding how many abstracts to return is.

**Q5.** Replace the fixed thresholds with a small claim-level model, trained on training claims, that predicts how many abstracts to return (0, 1 or 2) from cross-encoder and NLI features. 32 of the 40 remaining errors are about how many abstracts we return; only 6 have the wrong label. Multi-document claims fail most (8/12 wrong vs 21% overall). Simply splitting the threshold by claim group overfit in cross-validation (0.745 vs 0.751), so a learned model trained on the larger training split is needed.
