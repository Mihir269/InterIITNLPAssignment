# Report — SciFact retrieval and verification (all numbers: validation split)

**Q1.** BM25 wins on 673 (1.00 vs 0.36) and 1261 (1.00 vs 0.63): both claims contain rare exact terms that also appear in the gold title ("LDL-cholesterol", "reactive oxygen species", "genomic instability"); bge-base drifts to topically similar but broader reviews ("A Century of Cholesterol and Coronaries"). bge-base wins on 766 (1.00 vs 0.00; BM25 rank 67) and 1311 (1.00 vs 0.00): the gold abstract paraphrases the claim with no shared key word ("medications" ↔ "pharmacotherapy"; "lncRNAs" ↔ "LincRNA-p21", "trans-acting factors"). Overall 14 claims favour BM25, 16 favour bge-base, 76 tie.

**Q2.** Fusion. Min-max interpolation of BM25 (abstracts expanded with training claims), bge-base and MedCPT gave nDCG@10 0.949 vs 0.928 for the best single system (+0.021); without expansion 0.915 vs 0.898. Cost: ≈290 ms/query on CPU instead of 152 ms. Reranking added less: a general cross-encoder (bge-reranker-base) *hurt* (0.870). MedCPT-Cross-Encoder fed the citing training claims reached 0.956, and 0.968 after fine-tuning it on training claims (+0.019 over hybrid, p=0.10), at 9.2 s/query on CPU, about 30× the cost.

**Q3.** Yes. Fine-tuning bge-small (0.870 zero-shot) with hard negatives (BM25 ∪ base-model ranks 3–30, gold removed) gave 0.899, versus 0.882 with random negatives. I tracked validation nDCG@10 and nDCG@10 on 100 training claims after every epoch. Validation was still rising at epoch 4, so there was no clear overfitting, but a widening train–val gap (0.960 vs 0.899) and Recall@100 dropping 1.000 → 0.991 were early warning signs; the best epoch is selected on validation.

**Q4.** 4 of 43 validation error claims (9%) are retrieval_miss. Final verifier: two cross-encoders pick evidence among the hybrid's top 3; an NLI ensemble assigns the label (F1 0.771, 5-fold CV 0.751; NLI alone 0.627). Others: entity_mismatch 14, needs_multiple_docs 10, other 9, numerical 5, negation 1. By outcome: 15 claims miss a second abstract or add an extra one, 14 are false alarms on NEI claims, 8 miss all evidence, only 6 have the wrong label. Retrieval is not the bottleneck; deciding how many abstracts to return is.

**Q5.** Replace the fixed thresholds with a small claim-level model, trained on training claims, that predicts how many abstracts to return (0, 1 or 2) from cross-encoder and NLI features. 37 of the 43 remaining errors are about how many abstracts we return (missing second documents, NEI false alarms, misses), while only 6 have the wrong label. The current rule uses one global threshold tuned on 162 validation claims, and cross-validation already shows that is optimistic by about 0.02 F1.
