# Report — SciFact retrieval and verification (all numbers: validation split)

**Q1.** BM25 wins on 673 (1.00 vs 0.36) and 1261 (1.00 vs 0.63): both claims contain rare exact terms that also appear in the gold title ("LDL-cholesterol", "reactive oxygen species", "genomic instability"); bge-base drifts to topically similar but broader reviews ("A Century of Cholesterol and Coronaries"). bge-base wins on 766 (1.00 vs 0.00; BM25 rank 67) and 1311 (1.00 vs 0.00): the gold abstract paraphrases the claim with no shared key word ("medications" ↔ "pharmacotherapy"; "lncRNAs" ↔ "LincRNA-p21", "trans-acting factors"). Overall 14 claims favour BM25, 16 favour bge-base, 76 tie.

**Q2.** Fusion. Min-max interpolation of BM25 (abstracts expanded with training claims), bge-base and MedCPT reached nDCG@10 0.949 vs 0.928 for the best single system (+0.021; without expansion: 0.915 vs 0.898). Cost: one dense encode per extra model, ≈290 ms/query on CPU instead of 152 ms. The cross-encoder (bge-reranker-base) *hurt*: 0.870 at k=10, falling further with larger k, at 2.5 s/query on CPU. It demoted a correct #1 on 20 claims, usually in favour of a closely related study on the same topic (e.g. 34, 55): it scores topical relevance, not evidence, and cannot use the training-claim expansion.

**Q3.** Yes. Fine-tuning bge-small (0.870 zero-shot) with hard negatives (BM25 ∪ base-model ranks 3–30, gold removed) gave 0.899, versus 0.882 with random negatives. I tracked validation nDCG@10 and nDCG@10 on 100 training claims after every epoch. Validation was still rising at epoch 4, so there was no clear overfitting, but a widening train–val gap (0.960 vs 0.899) and Recall@100 dropping 1.000 → 0.991 were early warning signs; the best epoch is selected on validation.

**Q4.** 9 of 73 validation error claims (12%) are retrieval_miss (fine-tuned NLI on the hybrid retriever; hybrid Recall@3 = 0.94). The rest are verification errors: entity_mismatch 36 (mostly NEI claims where we confidently predicted an abstract), needs_multiple_docs 10, other 11, numerical 6, negation 1. Retrieval is not the bottleneck; deciding *whether* an abstract is evidence is.

**Q5.** Add a rationale-selection stage (SciFact-style): first predict which sentences are evidence, trained on the gold rationale sentences, and run the stance classifier only on those. False alarms on NEI claims are the largest error group (30 of 68 zero-shot errors), and they come from NLI firing on any single topically related sentence. A retrieval-score gate did not fix this: in 5-fold cross-validation on validation it scored 0.571 vs 0.593 without it, because it overfit the threshold.
